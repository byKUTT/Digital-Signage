# Signal Composition

Signal Composition treats every canvas as a field of deliberate visual priority. Space is functional: it separates price from product, date from event, and invitation from instruction before a viewer reads a word. Each composition should feel resolved from a distance and reward a closer look through exact alignment, measured proportion, and painstakingly tuned negative space.

Color carries the first message. Each family uses a compact palette with one decisive contrast rather than a collection of decorative accents. Large fields establish atmosphere while smaller chromatic interruptions indicate the one fact that matters most. The calibration must feel meticulously crafted, as though every relationship was tested repeatedly by a designer at the top of their field.

Typography behaves as architecture. Words are sparse, direct, and integrated into the geometry instead of filling containers with explanation. Scale establishes rhythm; weight establishes urgency. Landscape and portrait versions are separate compositions sharing the same visual grammar, never automatic crops of one another.

The system balances repeatability with character. A family shares material, type behavior, and visual cadence, while each variation changes hierarchy and spatial tension enough to serve a different message. The work should reveal deep expertise through restraint: fewer elements, better placed, with each edge, baseline, and interval receiving painstaking attention.

The final templates must feel like finished art objects that remain useful tools. Their craft comes from precision rather than ornament, and their flexibility comes from a clear underlying system rather than generic emptiness. Every template should look labored over for countless hours while still letting a user replace the words and publish confidently within minutes.
